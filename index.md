I am Jessica Sishi Fei, a Ph.D. student starting in 2025 in the Department of Human Development and Quantitative Methodology at the University of Maryland, College Park, where I work with Dr. Min Wang. My research examines how readers move from identifying individual words to constructing coherent representations of text, with a particular focus on readers who use English as a second language.

Comprehension requires more than recognizing words and accessing their meanings. As a text unfolds, readers must retrieve relevant information from the preceding text and integrate each incoming word into an evolving mental representation, thereby establishing coherence across sentences. My work examines how reader-level resources and text-level cues jointly shape how readers construct coherent meaning from text, and why this process varies across readers with different linguistic, cognitive, and developmental profiles.

I pursue this question through three complementary lines of work. The first examines the lexical foundations of comprehension, using meta-analytic structural equation modeling to test how word-level knowledge supports text-level comprehension. The second focuses on word-to-text integration during online reading, combining behavioral experiments with information-theoretic measures derived from neural language models. The third investigates how discourse cues, such as connectives, guide the coherence relations that readers establish across sentences and how these processes extend to written production.

Looking ahead, I am interested in connecting cognitive theories of reading with multilingual natural language processing. I plan to use multilingual language models to formalize the cognitive mechanisms underlying incremental meaning construction in first and second language reading, and to draw on human reading data to better understand how these models process language. I also hope to apply this mechanistic perspective to the design of AI-supported adaptive reading interventions for diverse learners. My broader goal is to translate a mechanistic understanding of reading comprehension into insights that can inform research and practice in language, literacy, and education. I am always happy to discuss these ideas and welcome opportunities for collaboration. Please feel free to reach out!
